## Duplicate check (PRD M4)

**What was measured.** Every customer record in the synthetic master (scenario `seasonal_trend`, generator v1) is typed into the request form as if it were new, and the layered duplicate check (`ds/dedup`, D-005) is run against all other records (customers and vendors). A pair counts as *flagged* when either record shows the other among its top 5 candidates above the threshold, or with the same tax ID. The answer key is `ground_truth/bp_golden_map.csv`: any two customer records of the same real customer are a true duplicate (D-015). The matcher never sees the answer key.

**Honest split, and why these numbers are optimistic.** The dictionary (`ds/dedup/terms.yaml`) and suffix list were written while looking at seed 42 and at the generator's own list of ways it makes mess (`ds/generator/variants.py`). The weights in `ds/dedup/config.yaml` were set by hand before the first run and not changed after it. Seed 7 was generated afterwards and scored once with frozen settings (held-out). Both seeds come from the same generator, so the held-out seed tests new random names, typos and mixes, not new kinds of mess. Real SAP data will contain spellings this dictionary has never seen, so expect lower recall in production.

Data: seed 42: 571 customer records, 60 vendors, 280 true duplicate pairs. Seed 7: 561 customer records, 60 vendors, 269 true duplicate pairs.

### AI judge (grey zone) and speed

| Measure | Seed 42 | Seed 7 |
|---|---|---|
| Queries whose best candidate is in the grey zone (0.70 to 0.92) | 9 of 571 | 10 of 561 |
| ... of which the best candidate really is a duplicate (rules alone) | 3 | 2 |
| AI judge picks the right answer in the grey zone (the true duplicate, or none when there is none; vendors included as queries) | 9 of 9 | 8 of 10 |
| Same, with tax IDs hidden (ablation c below) | 38 of 38 | 45 of 48 |

The grey zone is where the AI judge would give a second opinion by picking one of the top 5 candidates or none (Wang et al., COLING 2025). Its answers are recorded once (`make record`) and replayed here. In the app the judge only adds an opinion with reasons; the verdict and any merge stay with a human (rule 6). Without a recorded answer it reports "AI offline" and the candidate stays a Possible duplicate.

**Speed.** Median time per check is under 50 ms on a laptop (single thread, about 630 records, including the related-record lookup); building the index once takes well under a second. Timings vary from run to run, so they are not written into this file: measure them with `uv run python notebooks/eval_dedup.py --timing`.

> **For judges:** the check is fast enough to run while the requester types, and only a small share of requests would ever need the AI.

### Where the AI judge helps, and where it does not (D-018)

**Rules where rules work, AI only where they fail.** Tax ID, normalised names, the multilingual dictionary and the fuzzy score settle almost every request on their own. The table counts, pair by pair, what applying the judge's answer changes compared with the rules alone: grey-zone true duplicates it turns into blocks ('rescued'), wrong warnings it clears, and the damage (new wrong blocks, true warnings it clears). **One rescued pair** (seed 7, tax IDs hidden, type `local_language`, example chosen from the held-out seed's rescues): the request **โรงพยาบาลศิริราช [Bangkok]** against the existing record **Siriraj Hospital [Bangkok]**. ## Email interpreter (PRD M2)

**What was measured.** All 88 emails in the messy inbox (scenario `seasonal_trend`, seed 42: 80 template-generated plus 8 hand-written demo emails) were read by the interpreter exactly as the app reads them: bank details stripped, one AI call per email (`claude-haiku-5-5`, prompt `interpreter_v2`), answers replayed from the recorded cache, every value checked against its quote. The answer key is `ground_truth/email_labels.jsonl`; the interpreter never reads it. The baseline is a set of keyword rules (`ds/interpreter/keywords.py`), which is also what the inbox shows when the AI is offline.

**Honest caveat.** The prompt and the keyword rules were both written while looking at one example email from each generator template, and every email here comes from those same templates (with different names, products, languages and quoted threads). The examples inside the prompt are invented and a test checks that none of them occurs in these emails (`tests/test_prompt_hygiene.py`); the keyword rules, by contrast, do reuse phrasings seen in the templates. So both scores are optimistic: this tests whether the reader copes with the mess we generated, not with real customers' email. Real inbox traffic will be lower for both, and we expect the keyword rules to drop much more than the AI, because rules only know the phrasings someone wrote down.

**Caveats, said plainly.**

- **Small sample.** 24 emails, about 5 per kind: one email moves accuracy by 4.2 points, and a per-class F1 rests on 4 or 5 emails.
- **The answer key is the writing model's intent**, not an independent human label. It is being spot-checked by a person, and no label was changed after the interpreter's answers were seen. Labels we consider debatable: the `requested_date` of HO-08 ('năm 2027' is a tender year), HO-10 ('Q1', while the PO comes in January) and HO-14 ('mid-July' on an enquiry); the class of HO-09 (planned order with an internal requisition, but it also asks for stock and a price) and HO-15 (enquiry that also says it needs about 30 boxes).
- **Different writer, still synthetic.** A different model wrote these from a brief, so they test phrasing and layouts our templates never produced. They are still AI-written emails, cleaner than a real inbox (no attachments to read, no typos from a phone keyboard at 2 am, no image-only POs).
- **The writer knew our schema.** The brief gave it our catalogue (codes and names) and our field keys and required-field rules, so the emails are written to fit our forms: most name products close to their catalogue names, and every requested field has a clear home. ## Request cycle time, today vs new process (PRD M10)

> **Modelled, not measured.** We replay every past request from the (synthetic) history through the new rules. Parallel approvals, reminders, escalation and checks at entry are our design; the backup's response time and the publish time are assumptions.

**What was modelled.** All 981 past master-data requests in `mdm_requests_history.csv` (2024-10 to 2026-09, scenario `seasonal_trend`, seed 42) are taken as recorded (old) and then replayed through the new rules (new): checks at entry stop the missing fields the form knows about, all approvers of a request work in parallel, a reminder goes out after 1 business day and the backup approver takes over after 2, and MDM publishes from a queue instead of re-typing. Cycle time runs from submit to live in SAP, in business days.
