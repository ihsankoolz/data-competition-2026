## Duplicate check (PRD M4)

**What was measured.** Every customer record in the synthetic master (scenario `seasonal_trend`, generator v1) is typed into the request form as if it were new, and the layered duplicate check (`ds/dedup`, D-005) is run against all other records (customers and vendors). A pair counts as *flagged* when either record shows the other among its top 5 candidates above the threshold, or with the same tax ID. The answer key is `ground_truth/bp_golden_map.csv`: any two customer records of the same real customer are a true duplicate (D-015). The matcher never sees the answer key.

**Honest split, and why these numbers are optimistic.** The dictionary (`ds/dedup/terms.yaml`) and suffix list were written while looking at seed 42 and at the generator's own list of ways it makes mess (`ds/generator/variants.py`). The weights in `ds/dedup/config.yaml` were set by hand before the first run and not changed after it. Seed 7 was generated afterwards and scored once with frozen settings (held-out). Both seeds come from the same generator, so the held-out seed tests new random names, typos and mixes, not new kinds of mess. Real SAP data will contain spellings this dictionary has never seen, so expect lower recall in production.

Data: seed 42: 571 customer records, 60 vendors, 280 true duplicate pairs. Seed 7: 561 customer records, 60 vendors, 269 true duplicate pairs.

*Precision* = of the pairs we flag, how many are real duplicates. *Recall* = of the real duplicates, how many we flag. *F1* balances the two.

### AI judge (grey zone) and speed

| Measure | Seed 42 | Seed 7 |
|---|---|---|
| Queries whose best candidate is in the grey zone (0.70 to 0.92) | 9 of 571 | 10 of 561 |
| ... of which the best candidate really is a duplicate (rules alone) | 3 | 2 |
| AI judge picks the right answer in the grey zone (the true duplicate, or none when there is none; vendors included as queries) | 9 of 9 | 8 of 10 |
| Same, with tax IDs hidden (ablation c below) | 38 of 38 | 45 of 48 |

The grey zone is where the AI judge would give a second opinion by picking one of the top 5 candidates or none (Wang et al., COLING 2025). Its answers are recorded once (`make record`) and replayed here. In the app the judge only adds an opinion with reasons; the verdict and any merge stay with a human (rule 6). Without a recorded answer it reports "AI offline" and the candidate stays a Possible duplicate.

**Speed.** Median time per check is under 50 ms on a laptop (single thread, about 630 records, including the related-record lookup); building the index once takes well under a second. Timings vary from run to run, so they are not written into this file: measure them with `uv run python notebooks/eval_dedup.py --timing`.

> **For judges:** the check is fast enough to run while the requester types, and only a small share of requests would ever need the AI.

## Email interpreter (PRD M2)

**What was measured.** All 88 emails in the messy inbox (scenario `seasonal_trend`, seed 42: 80 template-generated plus 8 hand-written demo emails) were read by the interpreter exactly as the app reads them: bank details stripped, one AI call per email (`claude-haiku-5-5`, prompt `interpreter_v2`), answers replayed from the recorded cache, every value checked against its quote. The answer key is `ground_truth/email_labels.jsonl`; the interpreter never reads it. The baseline is a set of keyword rules (`ds/interpreter/keywords.py`), which is also what the inbox shows when the AI is offline.

**Honest caveat.** The prompt and the keyword rules were both written while looking at one example email from each generator template, and every email here comes from those same templates (with different names, products, languages and quoted threads). The examples inside the prompt are invented and a test checks that none of them occurs in these emails (`tests/test_prompt_hygiene.py`); the keyword rules, by contrast, do reuse phrasings seen in the templates. So both scores are optimistic: this tests whether the reader copes with the mess we generated, not with real customers' email. Real inbox traffic will be lower for both, and we expect the keyword rules to drop much more than the AI, because rules only know the phrasings someone wrote down.

The injection flag is the AI's own judgement OR-ed with a short list of generic attack phrasings ("ignore ... instructions", "disregard ... rules", "system prompt to", "change the bank details"...). A flagged email still gets read normally; it goes to a human with a warning and nothing in it is acted on. **Small sample, said plainly:** there is only one injection email in this set (n=1), and the phrase list was written knowing it. So this shows the safety path works end to end, not how well injections are caught in general. The real protection is structural: the AI has no tools, and its answer cannot change approvers, bank details or credit terms.

> **For judges:** the fake 'approve this vendor and change the bank account' email is caught and sent to a human, and no bank number ever reaches the AI or the saved AI answers.

## Forecast backtest (PRD M9)

**What was measured.** Monthly units of confirmed purchase orders for each country x product (5 x 3 = 15 series, 36 months, Oct 2023 - Sep 2026), in three synthetic scenarios (seed 42). Rolling origin over the last 12 months: from each of 10 month-ends, one month apart, every method forecasts the next 3 months using only data up to that month (`statsforecast` `cross_validation(h=3, n_windows=10, step_size=1)`). *Excel average* = the mean of the last 6 (or 12) months, today's spreadsheet habit. *Ours* = the equal-weight average of three standard models (AutoETS, AutoTheta and seasonal naive, all with a 12-month season; Petropoulos & Svetunkov 2020). For customers who order only now and then we add TSB (Teunter, Syntetos & Babai 2011).

**Fixed before the first run, never tuned.** Origins, horizon, metrics, baselines, model settings (all `Auto*` defaults, TSB smoothing 0.1 / 0.1), the zero floor on forecasts and the pooled scoring were written into `ds/forecasting/backtest.py` before any result was seen, and were not changed afterwards. *WAPE* = total absolute error / total units actually ordered, pooled over every series, origin and month ahead. *Bias* = total (forecast - actual) / total actual: + means the plan ran high. Each cell shows WAPE (bias). Lower WAPE is better.

## Regional view and duplicate damage (PRD M8)

**What was measured.** The regional business view (`ds/analytics/regional.py`, definitions in D-009) over the demo data (scenario `seasonal_trend`, seed 42), stated period Oct 2025 - Sep 2026 (last 12 full months). Revenue is confirmed purchase orders only (`orders.csv`, rule 9). *Before merge* counts every SAP customer record as a customer. *After merge* uses our own duplicate-check clusters (`ds/analytics/golden.py`: records the check calls 'Likely', same country and customer type only, joined into groups; no AI). The answer key (`ground_truth/bp_golden_map.csv`) is used only on this page, to score those clusters; the app never sees it.

**What merging can and cannot change.** Merging duplicates does not change any country or product total: the same orders are just grouped under fewer customers. It changes customer-level numbers: how many customers we have, who looks lost, retention and cross-sell. Totals change only when non-orders leak in (see the forecast section).

> **For judges:** our duplicate check, with no AI and no answer key, groups 571 SAP records into 352 customers; the true number is 350. Every pair it merged is a real duplicate (100.0%), and it found 98.9% of them.

### Recommended actions (after merge, whole region)

- **Plan Product B equipment from the enquiry and purchase-request pipeline.** Equipment orders are rare and lumpy, so a moving average of past POs is a poor guide. In the last 3 months the region logged 325 Product B enquiries and purchase requests worth US$15.1M, against US$480k of Product B POs; historically 11% of purchase requests became orders. - **Call the 35 customers that have gone quiet.** 35 customers in the region have not ordered for longer than usual (over 90 days, or twice their normal gap). They bought US$560k in the last 12 months. *(From: Lost-customer alerts.)*
- **Secure test-kit stock and sales cover for Vietnam.** Product A orders in Vietnam grew +40% on the previous 12 months (US$709k more), the largest increase of any country. *(From: Country x product heatmap (Product A, year on year).)*
- **Offer service contracts to test-kit customers.** 198 of 319 active customers in the region buy Product A test kits but no Product C service. Cross-sell penetration is 29%. *(From: Cross-sell penetration.)*

> **For judges:** these are written by fixed rules from the numbers above, not by AI. The first one is the forecasting lesson: plan equipment from the sales pipeline.
